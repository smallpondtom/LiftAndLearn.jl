Standard Operator Inference (OpInf)

Theory

If we consider a linear-quadratic system, the goal of Operator Inference is to non-intrusively obtain a reduced model of the form

\[ \dot{\hat{\mathbf x}}(t) = \hat{\mathbf A}\hat{\mathbf x}(t) + \hat{\mathbf{H}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \otimes \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)),\]

To do so, we will fit reduced operators $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$ to the reduced data in a least-squares sense. In addition to the state trajectory data

\[ \mathbf{X} = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf x}(t_1) & \mathbf{x}(t_2) & \cdots & \mathbf{x}(t_K) \\ | & | & & | \\ \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times K} ~,\]

we also require paired state time derivative data:

\[\begin{aligned} \dot{\mathbf{X}} = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t_1) & \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t_2) & \cdots & \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t_K) \\ | & | & & | \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times K} \quad \text{ where ~~} \dot{\mathbf x}(t_i)=\mathbf A\mathbf x(t_i)+ \mathbf H (\mathbf x(t_i) \otimes \mathbf x(t_i))~. \end{aligned}\]

This time derivative data can come directly from the simulation of the high-dimensional ODE or can be approximated numerically from the snapshot data. We use the POD basis $\mathbf{V}_r$ to compute reduced state and time derivative data as follows: let $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i = \mathbf{V}_r^\top\mathbf{x}(t_i)$ as an ansatz, and $\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}_i = \mathbf{V}_r^\top\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t_i)$ for $i=1,\ldots,K$. Then, define

\[ \hat{\textbf X} = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ \boldsymbol{\hat{\mathbf x}}_1 & \boldsymbol{ \hat{\mathbf x}}_2 & \cdots & \boldsymbol{\hat{\mathbf x}}_K \\ | & | & & | \\ \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb R^{r\times K}, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \dot{\hat{\textbf X}} = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ \dot{\hat{\mathbf x}}_1 & \dot{\hat{\mathbf x}}_2 & \cdots & \dot{\hat{\mathbf x}}_K \\ | & | & & | \\ \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb R^{r\times K}.\]

Additionally, we define the matrix formed by the quadratic terms of the state data

\[ \hat{\textbf X}_\otimes = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ (\hat{\mathbf x}_1 \otimes \hat{\mathbf x}_1) & (\hat{\mathbf x}_2 \otimes \hat{\mathbf x}_2) & \cdots & (\hat{\mathbf x}_K \otimes \hat{\mathbf x}_K) \\ | & | & & | \\ \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb R^{r^2\times K}.\]

This allows us to formulate the following minimization for finding the reduced operators $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{H}}$:

\[ \textbf{Standard OpInf}: \qquad \min_{\mathbf{\hat{A}}\in\mathbb R^{r\times r},~\hat{\mathbf{H}}\in\mathbb R^{r\times r^2}} \sum_{i=1}^K \left\| \dot{\hat{\mathbf x}}_i - \mathbf{\hat{A}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i - \hat{\mathbf H}(\hat{\mathbf x}_i \otimes \hat{\mathbf x}_i) \right \|^2_2 = \min_{\mathbf{O}\in\mathbb R^{r\times(r+r^2)}} \|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{O}^\top - \dot{\hat{\mathbf X}}\|_F^2,\]

where $\mathbf D = [\hat{\mathbf X}^\top, ~\hat{\mathbf X}_\otimes^\top] \in \mathbb R^{K\times(r+r^2)}$ and $\mathbf O = [\mathbf{\hat A}, ~\hat{\mathbf H}] \in \mathbb R^{r\times(r+r^2)}$.

Implementation

In this package we implement the standard OpInf along with Tikhonov regularized version with the function opinf.

There are many things going on under the hood when the function:

  • constructing the data matrix
  • construting the Tikhonov matrix
  • reprojection

But all of those operations are taken care of automatically. For full details please see the source code.

LiftAndLearn.opinf — Function
opinf(X, Vn, options; U, Y, Xdot)

Infer the reduced operators by Operator Inference (OpInf) from full-order state data.

The state data is projected onto the basis Vn ($\hat{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{V}_n^\top\mathbf{X}$). If Xdot is empty, the time-derivative data is approximated from X by finite differences with time_derivative_approx using options.data.deriv_type and options.data.Δt, and the state, input and output data are restricted to the time indices returned by the scheme. The regression is then built with get_data_matrix and solved with leastsquares_solve.

Arguments

  • X::AbstractArray: full state snapshot matrix of size $n \times K$ (rows = state, columns = time).
  • Vn::AbstractArray: basis (e.g. POD basis) of size $n \times r$.
  • options::AbstractOption: OpInf options (typically an LSOpInfOption).

Keyword Arguments

  • U::AbstractArray=[0.0]: input data ($K \times m$ or $m \times K$; fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Y::AbstractArray=[0.0]: output data ($K \times l$ or $l \times K$; fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Xdot::AbstractArray=[]: full time-derivative data of size $n \times K$; if empty it is approximated by finite differences.

Returns

  • op::Operators: the inferred reduced operators.

opinf(Xhat, options; U, Y, Xhatdot)

Infer the operators by Operator Inference (OpInf) directly from reduced (already projected) state and derivative data.

Arguments

  • Xhat::AbstractArray: reduced state snapshot matrix of size $r \times K$ (rows = reduced state, columns = time).
  • options::AbstractOption: OpInf options (typically an LSOpInfOption).

Keyword Arguments

  • U::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): input data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Y::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): output data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Xhatdot::AbstractArray=[]: reduced time-derivative data of size $r \times K$; required.

Returns

  • op::Operators: the inferred operators.

Notes

  • Xhatdot must be provided; an error is thrown if it is empty.

opinf(X, Vn, full_op, options; U, Y, return_derivative)

Infer the reduced operators by Operator Inference (OpInf) with the reprojection scheme.

Instead of using finite differences, the derivative data is generated by evaluating the full-order model full_op at the reconstructed reduced states and projecting back onto Vn (see reproject).

Arguments

  • X::AbstractArray: full state snapshot matrix of size $n \times K$ (rows = state, columns = time).
  • Vn::AbstractArray: basis (e.g. POD basis) of size $n \times r$.
  • full_op::Operators: full-order model operators.
  • options::AbstractOption: OpInf options (typically an LSOpInfOption).

Keyword Arguments

  • U::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): input data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Y::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): output data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • return_derivative::Bool=false: also return the reprojected derivative data $\mathbf{R}^\top$.

Returns

  • op::Operators: the inferred reduced operators.
  • Rt::AbstractArray: reprojected derivative data of size $K \times r$ (only if return_derivative=true).

Notes

  • reproject reads one input row per snapshot, so U must have $K$ time samples (pass zeros for an autonomous system); the default zeros(1,1) only works for $K = 1$.

opinf(W, Vn, lm, full_op, options; U, Y)

Infer the reduced operators of a lifted system by Lift & Learn (LnL).

If options.optim.reproject is true, the derivative data is generated by reprojection through the lifting map (see the lifting method of reproject); otherwise it is approximated from W by finite differences with time_derivative_approx, and the state, input and output data are restricted to the time indices of the scheme.

Arguments

  • W::AbstractArray: lifted state snapshot matrix of size $n \times K$ (rows = lifted state, columns = time).
  • Vn::AbstractArray: basis of the lifted state of size $n \times r$ (e.g. from lifted_basis).
  • lm::lifting: lifting map.
  • full_op::Operators: full-order operators of the original (unlifted) model (used for reprojection).
  • options::AbstractOption: OpInf options (typically an LSOpInfOption).

Keyword Arguments

  • U::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): input data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).
  • Y::AbstractArray=zeros(1,1): output data (fat matrices are transposed to tall).

Returns

  • op::Operators: the inferred reduced operators.

Notes

  • Use the unlifted opinf(X, Vn, options) method if reprojection is not needed.
  • With reprojection, U must have $K$ time samples (pass zeros for an autonomous system); the default zeros(1,1) only works for $K = 1$.
